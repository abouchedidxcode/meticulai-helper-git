# Testing your game

Your play is the most important part of this whole process. The agent can build, measure and review, but it can't
feel whether a game is fun. You can. You don't need any experience to playtest; you just need to be honest.

## 1. Click the link

Every time the agent hands back, its message starts with:

```
▶ Play it now: http://localhost:5173
```

Click it. The game opens in your browser. ("localhost" means the game is running on your own computer.) If the link
doesn't open, tell the agent "the link doesn't work". Don't try to fix it yourself.

## 2. Play like a player

- **Play first, read later.** Don't read the agent's notes before your first minute. A real player won't have them.
- **Don't explain the game to yourself.** If you didn't know what to do, that's the finding. Write it down; don't
  excuse it ("well, I know the agent meant...").
- **Notice your feelings.** When did you smile? When did you get bored? When were you confused? When did you want
  another go?
- **Play for two or three minutes.** Long enough to get past the start, short enough to remember it.

## 3. Press N to leave a note

Press **N** at any moment in the game. A small box opens. Type what you noticed and press Enter. It saves your note
together with a screenshot of that exact moment, and the agent reads all notes at the start of its next turn.

Leave notes at the moment something happens. "Here the jump felt floaty" with a screenshot beats a summary later.

## What's useful to write

Useful:
- "I didn't know what to do for the first 20 seconds."
- "Landing on the platform felt great, the thud is perfect."
- "I died and didn't know why."
- "Bored here. Nothing new for a minute."
- "The colours are too bright, it hurts my eyes."
- "This is the best moment so far. More of this."

Less useful:
- "Make it better." (Better how? When?)
- "Add 10 levels and a shop." (Ask for a feeling first: "I want a reason to keep playing after level 2.")
- Long lists of tiny fixes. Your top one or two things matter most.

Say what you liked too. The agent will protect what you praise and stop changing it.

## The "Try this" missions

The agent's message includes a short "Try this" list. Those point you straight at what changed, so you can judge it.
Do them after your first free minute of play.

## Test mode: skip buttons

Sometimes the agent gives a second link ending in `?test=1`. That's test mode: skip buttons to jump straight to a
level or moment, and a small card showing the controls. Use it to check one thing quickly without replaying the
start. Play normally (the first link) for your overall impression.

## The dev menu: change the game while it runs

Press the **`** key (top left of the keyboard, under Esc; **F2** works too) while the game is open. A panel slides in
on the right. Press the same key again to close it. Only you see it: people who play your finished game never do.

- **Essentials** is a short list of the things that matter most, in plain words: how fast you run, how high you
  jump, how far back the camera sits. Drag a slider while you play and feel the difference at once. A changed row
  turns gold; double-click it to put it back.
- **Everything** shows every number the game has. Type in the search box to find one.
- **Presets** are whole feels to try ("Floaty", "Heavy"...). Hover one to read what it does.
- **A B C**: click "keep", then A, to keep what you have; change things; click A again to flip back and compare.
- **Save for my agent** writes what you chose into the game. Tell the agent you saved; it will keep the values.

Tools at the top, for a closer look:
- **Freeze** stops time (**Step** moves it on a tiny bit). **Speed** plays it slower or faster.
- **Camera** lets you fly round the scene: W A S D to move, Q and E for down and up, drag to look, the mouse wheel to
  zoom. Press C or Esc to go back. The game doesn't notice.
- **No HUD** hides the score and buttons for a clean picture. **Screenshot** saves one for the agent.
- **Computer plays** lets the computer play while you watch and change things.
- **Skip to** buttons jump straight to a moment (the last level, the boss...).

Lower down: **Look** has the game's colours, the light and the sun, and the **Vibe** chooser, which tries whole
ready-made looks. Brightness changes at once; for the rest press **Save palette for my agent** and the game reloads
with the new colours. **Sound** has the volumes and a Mute switch.

## Getting someone else to play

The best test of all: sit a friend down, give them the game, **say nothing**, and watch. Note where they get stuck,
where they smile, and when they put it down. Tell the agent. (The localhost link only works on your computer. To send
it to someone far away, ask the agent to help you share it.)
