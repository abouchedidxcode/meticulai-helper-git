# Free assets

## What they are

"Assets" are the pieces a game is made of besides the code: 3D models (a rock, a tree, a character), textures (the
surface of wood or stone), skies, little pictures for 2D games, sound effects, music and fonts.

People and studios around the world make these and give them away. Some libraries have thousands of them, all free,
and many under a licence called **CC0**, which means "do anything you like with it". The kit knows where these
libraries are and how to search them.

The kit also has **ready-made effects and systems**, called blocks: fire, an ocean, a sky with clouds, trees, rain,
magic effects, crowds that walk around. They are free code written by other people that your agent can drop into your
game and then adjust.

## Why your agent uses them first

- **It's faster.** A good free rock takes a minute to find. Making one from scratch takes much longer.
- **It's free.** Generating pictures or models with a paid service costs money. Free assets cost nothing.
- **It often looks better.** Many free assets are made by skilled artists or scanned from real objects.

Your agent doesn't just grab the first thing it finds. It picks one style for the whole game, looks at each asset
before using it, and changes colours and sizes so everything looks like it belongs in the same game. When nothing free
fits, it makes the thing in code, and only after that would it suggest paying for generation (always asking you first).

## What "credit" means

Some free assets ask that you name the person who made them, like a thank-you in the credits of a film. That's called
giving credit.

**You don't have to do anything.** Your agent writes every credit into a file called `CREDITS.md` in your game
folder, at the moment each asset comes in. If an asset needs credit, it also goes on the credits screen in your game.
You can open `CREDITS.md` any time to see where everything came from.

## Saying "I don't like that one"

Just say it, in your own words: "that tree looks too cartoony", "the music is too sad", "the rocks don't match".
Your agent will try another option or look in a different style.

For the things that set the look of your game (your character, the main object, the sky, the main music), your agent
shows you pictures of two or three options and says which one it recommends. You pick. For small things (a pebble, a
click sound) it decides and tells you.

## Never use

- Anything marked **non-commercial**, "personal use only" or "no derivatives" (not allowed to change it). Your agent
  checks this for you.
- Art, music or characters from other games, films or brands, even fan-made copies. Your game should be yours.
- Anything with no clear licence. If nobody can say what you're allowed to do with it, don't use it.

If you have a picture or a sound you made yourself and want in the game, drop it in your game folder and tell your
agent. Your own work is always fine.
